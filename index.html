The shark's brain

Nineteen senses, twelve hidden units, two decisions: how hard to turn, and how hard to climb or dive. Filled circles are firing now; line weight is the strength of a connection. At stage 8 the hidden units start feeding back into themselves, and the brain gains a memory of the tick before.

Point at a sense, or at turn or climb, to see it in the tank.

Directions are the shark's own: "right" is where a positive turn takes it, "above" is where a positive climb takes it. Sight reaches 160 units and stops, in a 150° cone. The six wall senses carry at any distance, so a shark with nothing in view never reads a constant. The last four senses are the nose: how strong the scent is here, whether there is more of it to the right or above, and whether it is building up ahead.

What each faculty is worth

Each generation's champion hunts the same water up to three times: intact, with the four scent inputs forced to zero, and with its memory held at zero. Same brain, same fish, same start. The school is adapting too, so expect these to wander.

The school

Every fish carries seven inherited numbers. When one is eaten, its place is taken by a mutated copy of a survivor, so the school is under selection the whole time the shark is hunting it. Tick marks are where each trait started.

A fish that is bolting is not feeding. When a place falls empty it goes to a survivor chosen in proportion to how well it has been eating, so watchfulness is paid for in descendants. That bill is the only thing stopping the school from becoming one of paranoid sprinters.

Keep this brain

Training starts from nothing every time you open this page. Save the weights and you can carry a hunter between sessions, or take it apart offline.

The school is Reynolds' boids in three dimensions: separate, align, follow the centre, and flee a predator. Those weights are no longer fixed — each fish inherits its own, so the school drifts as the shark improves and the shark's catch falls as the school does. Neither side settles.

The seventh number is the one to watch: how far off a straight line away from the shark a fish breaks. Nothing charges for it. Breaking sideways keeps you nearer the thing chasing you, and breaking too far points you back into it, so the price is paid in geometry rather than in energy. Which way round a fish breaks is set at birth and not inherited, which is why a school bursts apart instead of wheeling as one.

Every fish leaves scent in the water each tick. The field diffuses in all three directions and decays, so the cloud behind a school is a record of where it has just been. The shark reads it by comparing four nostrils, not by being told a direction.

The brain is a 19–12–2 network with tanh units and 266 weights, twelve of which give each hidden unit a leaky connection to its own state one tick earlier — a time constant, so it can average a signal over time instead of only reacting to this instant. Until stage 8 those are held at zero. No gradients and no machine-learning library: weights are mutated and selected on how many fish each shark catches. Senses are handed over one stage at a time, because a brain given everything at once tends to learn nothing.

Because both sides move, a generation's catch is not comparable with one fifty generations earlier: a later shark may be better and still eat less. The trait chart is the other half of that story.

Training runs in a background thread, so the tank stays smooth while the shark learns; pausing the water only freezes the view. The 3D drawing uses three.js; everything that learns is the plain JavaScript in this file.
